# Starfall '89 art direction

Players sit close to a luminous screen, so the playfield uses a dark indigo sky with muted terrain. Peach is friendly fire, coral is danger, ice cyan is the player and green marks repair pickups. Cream lettering belongs to the physical instruction surround. Use crisp authored pixel sprites, bitmap title lettering, thin frame dividers, restrained chamfered controls. No decorative glow or blur. The wide playfield dominates; instructions form a narrow right rail that moves below on mobile. Start, pause, death, victory and stress states stay legible above the game. Reduced motion removes screen shake and decorative pulses.

Runtime artwork lives in `assets/`: a reduced 256 × 128 atlas, its frame/pivot/animation metadata, and the 640 × 400 background, adapted from the prepared Starfall pack. Only player, chaser, gunship, boss, friendly bolt, enemy round, repair, thrust, explosion and impact frames are shipped. Repair uses the supplied green capsule; shield and bomb pickup artwork is excluded.

`sprites.ts` draws native frame dimensions with nearest sampling and transparent edge padding. Ships rotate around their center; thrust rotates with the player around its top attachment. Collision circles and targeting remain independent of sprite silhouettes, including the broad boss hull. The boss health bar sits above its artwork. Responsive canvas scaling remains pixelated; non-integer CSS scales and arbitrary ship rotations can produce uneven pixel steps.

The supplied background is not seamless: draw it once in screen space, darkened behind gameplay, never tiled or scrolled. Sparse stars and debris provide movement. Effects stay below ships, bullets and pickups. Thrust loops at 80 ms per frame; explosions play four 80 ms frames; impacts last 80 ms. Their scene-owned fixed-update clock continues through hit-stop, pauses with overlays, and clears on replacement/disposal. Effects consume no simulation randomness and are capped at 128 concurrent bursts. Reduced motion fixes thrust to its first frame, replaces expanding explosions with a small static impact, freezes decorative background movement, and suppresses shake and screen flashes.
